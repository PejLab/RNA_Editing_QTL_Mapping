**Fig. 1 | cis-RNA editing QTLs across 49 GTEx tissues.**
**a**, Example edQTL. Editing level (edited/total reads; samples with ≥20 reads) at an edited site in *NEIL1* (chr15:75,353,371) by genotype of the lead variant chr15:75,336,500 (C>G) in brain cerebellum (q = 6.7 × 10⁻⁵¹). Boxes show the median and interquartile range; whiskers extend to 1.5× the interquartile range.
**b**, Number of edSites (editing sites with a cis-edQTL at permutation-based FDR ≤ 5%) against sample size for 49 tissues (73–702 samples); 104,143 edSites in total (32,308 unique sites). Dashed line, linear fit; r, Pearson correlation.
**c**, Position of the lead edQTL variant relative to the edited site for edSites in brain and other tissues (2.5-kb bins, ±100 kb cis window); 50% of lead variants lie within 10 kb of the site.
**d**, Absolute effect size of the lead variant (normalized editing level per alternative allele) against minor allele frequency for all edSites; color shows the number of edSites (log scale), the line the median per frequency bin. Larger effects at low frequency partly reflect lower power to detect small effects of rare variants.
**e**, Sharing of edQTL effects between tissues. For each pair of tissues, the Pearson correlation between the effects of each tissue's lead edQTLs and the effects of the same variants on the same sites in the other tissue (nominal statistics; average of both directions; tissues ordered by hierarchical clustering; top bar, brain tissues). Across 2.6 million lookups, 92% of effects have the same direction.
**f**, Fraction of edSites with two or more conditionally independent edQTLs (stepwise regression) against sample size; 5.0% across all tissues.
cis-edQTLs were mapped with tensorQTL (±100 kb window) using 3 genotype PCs, 5 phenotype PCs, sex and age as covariates. Editing levels were normalized by quantile normalization across samples followed by a rank-based inverse normal transform of each site, after regressing out the expression of each site's host gene(s) (both strands; GTEx v8 TPM), as in Li et al. 2022.
